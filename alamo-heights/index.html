What working the Heights tends to look like

Many homes in and around Alamo Heights are older, some dating back to the 1920s. That keeps a steady stream of work going for plumbers, electricians, remodelers, foundation and roofing crews, tree and lawn care, and house cleaners. Broadway is the main commercial strip, lined with restaurants, salons, and shops. Austin Highway and Basse Road add more storefronts and service businesses nearby.

Those customers usually care about the details. They read reviews, look at photos of your work, and check whether you list the area they live in. Getting the basics right goes a long way: clear service categories, real photos, accurate hours, and a service area that names Alamo Heights, Olmos Park, and Terrell Hills if you work there.

If print is part of your plan, a small, tidy postcard or door hanger run aimed at a few streets can fit this area better than a big blast.

FAQ

Alamo Heights is its own city. Does that change anything on Google?

It can affect how customers describe where they live. If you serve Alamo Heights, Olmos Park, and Terrell Hills, it's worth naming them in your service area and on your website so customers can tell you cover them.

My business gets most of its work from referrals. Do I still need this?

Usually, yes. Most referred customers still look you up before they call. A thin or outdated listing can cost you a job someone else already sent your way.

I work out of a truck, not an office. How should my Google listing be set up?

Many contractors here work out of a truck, not a storefront. Google's rules say service-area businesses should hide their address and list the areas they serve instead. If you work in Alamo Heights, Olmos Park, and Terrell Hills, I'd name all three so customers in 78209 can see you cover them.
